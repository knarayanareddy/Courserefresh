# Learner telemetry & the consent wall

**Learning objective.** Define the two stuck signals and what each means; explain consent-first storage — no consent row means nothing is stored, and the refusal is itself receipted.

## 1. The promise that justifies watching

The brief the whole case study answers has two halves: "update as the subject
changes" (Modules II–IV) and "**spot when a learner is stuck before they ask**"
(this module). Watching learners is the most ethically loaded thing this course
builds — which is why it gets the strictest architecture in the repository: watch
two signals, store almost nothing, and touch a human only with recorded consent,
under caps that live in code.

The signals, defined before any data arrives:

1. **`consecutive_wrong ≥ 2`** — two wrong answers in a row, within one lesson
   session, on items tagged with the same concept. Interpretation: the learner is
   acting on a misconception, not guessing (one wrong answer is noise; two in a
   row on the *same concept* is a pattern with a name).
2. **`dwell ≥ 3× the learner's own median`** — time on a section at triple their
   personal median without reaching an attempt. Interpretation: the section lost
   them (or delighted them — the signal is a *moment for help, not a verdict*;
   the design vocabulary is deliberately non-judgmental: stuck, never "struggling"
   or "failing").

Both thresholds are **per-learner**, not cohort-global: a fast reader's 3× and a
careful reader's 3× are different wall-clocks, which suppresses the classic
"penalising thoroughness" false positive.

## 2. The consent wall

The rule is one sentence and the architecture is absolutely literal about it:

> **Consent precedes storage. No consent row → no storage, no contact — and the
> refusal itself is receipted.**

```
telemetry POST {learner_handle, quiz_attempt · dwell, consent: true|false|absent}
  → consent false/absent: HTTP 403 · receipt [consent_missing] · nothing stored
  → consent true:         HTTP 200 · row stored · receipt written
```

Design details that make it a *wall* and not a policy statement:

- **Fail-closed.** No row matches "not consented". Missing, malformed, stale —
  all refuse. The wall's default is no. (This is Lecture 07's unknown-state
  posture applied to people instead of data.)
- **The refusal is an artifact.** When a stuck learner without consent would have
  been helped, the refusal receipt (`consent_missing`) proves the wall held —
  *auditable ethics*. The running repo's hero run contains exactly this receipt,
  preserved deliberately: block, receipt, silence toward the human.
- **Consent is versioned and pseudonymous.** The consent record carries its form
  version hash; handles are hashed (`learner:<sha256[..8]>`) — *no names in logs,
  receipts, digests, or the demo, ever*. The ask itself is a ≤45-second script
  including the stop mechanism, what's tracked (quiz attempts, time), what
  happens to data (deleted at pilot end / 14 days), and that nothing is graded.
- **Opt-out changes nothing about access.** Refusing telemetry never costs a
  learner the course. The refusal is a *normal outcome, reported as a count*
  (`telemetry_rejected`) — not a failure to convert.

## 3. What is stored — and never stored

Stored per consented learner, the minimum the signals need: handle, attempts
(concept, right/wrong, ts), dwell samples, consent version, message caps state.
**Never stored:** names, contact details, grades as scores (micro-lessons are
never graded — §V of the plan), free-text anything, or anything the two signals
do not strictly need. Aggregates print only at n ≥ 3; a delta is never shown
without its n and window (Lecture 18's honesty rule, generalised to people).

The deletion rule is boring and absolute: 14 days or pilot end, whichever first,
plus deletion on request at any time. Boring is the point — the ethics lives in
the schema, not in a privacy policy PDF nobody reads.

## 3b. The ethics floor, restated as code

Because the learner loop is the risk, its guardrails exist as testable config,
not culture:

1. Consent precedes storage (fail-closed, receipted refusals)
2. Handles hashed at capture; no names anywhere, including the demo
3. Caps in code: 1 message/day, 3/week, 1 per concept per 7 days — *never* in copy
4. Aggregates at n ≥ 3 only; every delta with its n and window
5. Delete at 14 days / pilot end / on request
6. Leaving costs nothing (opt-out preserves access)

The "never do" list from the plan is short enough to memorise and absolute: no
grading, no profiling, no engagement nudges, no third-party sharing, no contact
without consent — and no exceptions "for the demo". The demo *is* where the
pressure to except arrives; the wall holds or the course's own constitution is
a costume.

## 4. The loop this enables

With consent rows in place, the LEARN workflow (schedule: every 15 min) runs the
stuck-check over the telemetry window: signal fired → caps checked (Lecture 20's
rate limits) → micro-lesson dispatched (Lecture 20's format) → dispatch
receipted. When a publish is in flight, the same workflow evaluates the revert
gates (Lecture 18). One schedule, two duties: *help the stuck, honour the
promises.*

Notice the symmetry with the change loop: both loops are NOTICE→…→REPORT
subsets with receipts at every step; the learner loop just notices *people*
instead of *pages*. The architecture is deliberately identical — because the
disciplines (consent in, evidence out, caps in code, refusals receipted) are the
same disciplines.

## 5. Common mistakes

1. **Storage first, consent later.** "We'll pseudonymise at analysis time" — the
   row already existed with a name on it. The wall is at the boundary or nowhere.
2. **Global cohort thresholds.** 3× the *cohort* median flags every careful
   reader. Personal medians, or don't ship the signal.
3. **The graded nudge.** Any message that implies evaluation ("you're behind")
   converts help into pressure and violates the format contract. The dispatch is
   one micro-lesson, neutral, capped.
4. **Unreceipted refusals.** A consent wall that blocks silently cannot prove it
   held. The refusal receipt is the proof — without it, ethics claims are
   sincerity, not evidence.

## Recap and practice

You can define both signals with their per-learner semantics, recite the consent
wall with its fail-closed default, and list the six guardrails as testable rules.

**Practice:** (a) A learner consents Tuesday, opts out Thursday; a stuck signal
fires Friday. What may the system store, what may it send, what does the receipt
say? *(Store nothing new — the consent row is gone/no longer valid; send nothing;
receipt [consent_missing] or [consent_withdrawn]; the learner keeps full course
access — the wall respects time, not just presence.)* (b) Write your capstone's
consent ask verbatim (≤45 seconds of speech), and say where your `consent_missing`
receipt is written — *before* you build anything that stores telemetry.
